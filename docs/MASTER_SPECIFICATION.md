3. MAIN STORY AND NARRATIVE
==================================================

The player receives an unusual letter from an unknown sender.

The letter describes a forgotten town that disappeared from the world many years ago.

The sender claims that the town contains a secret capable of changing everything the player believes about reality.

The player travels to the town.

Upon arrival, something is deeply wrong.

- Every clock has stopped.
- The railway station is abandoned.
- Doors are locked.
- Houses are empty.
- Strange symbols appear on walls.
- Letters describe events that have not happened yet.
- Some rooms appear different when revisited.
- A mysterious presence seems to observe the player.

The player must investigate locations, collect evidence, solve interconnected puzzles, discover hidden objects, unlock secret areas, and reconstruct the forgotten history.

Gradually, the player discovers that the town is connected to an extraordinary experiment involving time, memory, identity, and reality.

The central mystery must contain multiple revelations.

Important narrative principles:
- Create memorable characters.
- Give each major character a believable history.
- Use environmental storytelling.
- Build suspense gradually.
- Introduce unexpected but logical plot twists.
- Make discoveries emotionally meaningful.
- Connect early clues to late-game revelations.
- Avoid predictable storytelling.
- Ensure that the final revelation changes the player's understanding of earlier events.

Do not reveal the entire mystery too early.

6. PUZZLE DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

Create at least 50 original puzzles distributed across all seven chapters.

Do not create 50 disconnected password-entry questions.

Each major puzzle must be part of a logical dependency chain.

Example:

The player finds a broken clock in the manor.

The clock requires a missing mechanical component.

The component is located in a locked storage room.

The storage room requires a combination.

The combination is hidden in a diary.

The diary contains a reference to a painting.

The painting contains the actual clue.

The player must investigate multiple locations before solving the original problem.

Every puzzle must have:
- A clear objective.
- A logical solution.
- Discoverable clues.
- An interactive interface.
- A success animation.
- An appropriate sound effect.
- A meaningful gameplay reward.
- A properly recorded completion state.

Prevent softlocks.

Never require an item that can become permanently inaccessible.

Provide three hint levels:

Level 1: A subtle hint.
Level 2: A more direct explanation.
Level 3: A strong hint guiding the player toward the solution.

Hints should not automatically reveal the complete answer unless explicitly requested.
